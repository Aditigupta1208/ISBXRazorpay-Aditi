# Merchant conversations: exact questions

Goal: three to five conversations of 30 to 40 minutes. Each one should leave you with one quote you can use and one product choice it supports or changes. Ask about the last real dispute, not about hypotheticals.

## Who to talk to

Anyone at an Indian business that sells services, subscriptions, travel or digital goods to customers abroad and has had at least one card dispute: founders, finance or support leads at SaaS companies, tour operators, agencies, course sellers. A payments or chargeback specialist counts as a sixth conversation, not a replacement. If they have never had a dispute, use the short version at the end.

## Opening (2 minutes)

"I'm building a prototype for a case competition. I'm not selling anything and nothing you say will be quoted with your name. I'd like to hear how disputes really go for you. There are no right answers. Is it OK if I take notes?"

## Part 1: the last dispute (15 minutes)

1. Tell me about the last card dispute you got from a customer abroad. What happened, start to finish? *(Let them talk. Do not suggest.)*
2. How did you first find out, and how long did you have to respond?
3. Who was involved, and who made the decision to fight or accept?
4. What did you decide, and how did you decide? What did you look at?
5. Where did the evidence come from? Which of it was in one place, and which was in someone else's hands (a developer, a booking tool, WhatsApp, email)?
6. How long did it take you to gather it? What was the hardest document to get?
7. What did you submit, and how did you write the response? Who wrote it?
8. What happened in the end? Did you win or lose, and how did you find out?
9. Looking back, was fighting worth it? What did it cost you in time and money?
10. Do you accept some disputes without looking? Which ones, and why?
11. What did you change afterwards, in your checkout, your terms or your process? Did anything change?

## Part 2: what they use today (5 minutes)

12. What do you use now, if anything, to handle disputes: Razorpay's dashboard, spreadsheets, a person, an agency? What do you like about it? What annoys you?
13. If you could have one thing done for you on a dispute, what would it be?

## Part 3: the prototype (10 minutes)

Share the link and ask them to think aloud. Do not explain the screens.

14. Open the list. Which dispute would you open first, and why? *(Tests whether the deadline and call are clear.)*
15. Open C06. What does this tell you to do? Would you do it? What would you want to see before you did? *(Tests the call and the cited draft.)*
16. Open C15. What do you make of "Escalate"? Would you send the message? *(Tests whether Escalate is understood and trusted.)*
17. "Test it on the bank first": would you use this before submitting? What would you expect it to tell you?
18. If this said Fold on a dispute you were sure you could win, what would you do?
19. What would make you not trust this?
20. Would you let it message you on WhatsApp when a deadline is close, with the response already drafted? What would you not want it to do on its own?

## Close (2 minutes)

21. Who else should I talk to?
22. Is there anything I didn't ask that I should have?

## What each answer tells you

| If you hear | It points to |
|---|---|
| "I accept everything, I can't find the documents" | Evidence collection is the real problem. Move document gathering and the "get this first" message up. |
| "I fight if it's over a certain amount" | The money line is the core. Make the rupee threshold the first thing on the page. |
| "I don't trust a tool to say Fold" | Keep Accept as one click with a visible reason. Soften nudges. |
| "I'd never act on a WhatsApp message" | Cut or reframe Deadline rescue. |
| "I don't know why I lost" | The Results page and the bank test matter more. |
| "I changed my checkout after a loss" | The policy patch idea has real demand. Build it. |

## Short version (no disputes yet, or a payments expert)

Ask 1 (about the last time a customer asked for a refund or questioned a charge), 5, 7, 9, 12, 13, then 14 to 16 on the prototype.

## Quote rules

Write down exact words. Ask before you use a quote. Remove names and business names unless they say yes. Use at most one quote per product choice, and say "one of N merchants" in the pitch, never "merchants say".

## Round 3 (asked 9 Oct): how they would use it

A short follow-up with the same three merchants, after the first two rounds. The exact wording was typed in chat and not saved, so this lists the topics, not the questions word for word. What they answered is in `docs/MERCHANT_FINDINGS.md`, "Round 3".

| Topic | What it was meant to test |
|---|---|
| How you would want to hear about a new dispute, and which ones you would want to hear about | Whether the way in is an alert or a dashboard, and what rules decide it |
| What you check before approving a drafted response | Which facts a merchant verifies by hand (amounts, dates, counts, scope, logs) |
| What would make you stop trusting it | The trust-breakers: one wrong fact, slowness, long text, an unasked refund |
| What you would want it to never do for you | Whether "never send, never refund, never accept for me" matches their own limits |
| What you would do if it said Fold, and what you would want to see | Whether a Fold must name the missing document or the rule, and how they read percentages |
| What you do when the missing document cannot arrive in time | Submit what you have, ask the customer or client, or fold |
| Why you fight, and when you do not | Protecting a record or ratio, precedent, or personal amount thresholds |
| Whether you can see your dispute ratio today | Whether a ratio number would be used |
| Who sees a draft before you approve it | Whether a "copy a summary" step is needed |
